StoreKit

        One storefront engine. Eight live brands.
        
Every supplement storefront needs the same surfaces: hero, catalog, product page, cart, checkout, account, proof of testing, compliance gates. Rebuilding them per brand is slow, and copying one brand's site into another leaves colours and images bleeding through.

        
StoreKit is a tokenized component system where a brand is one file. Edit tokens.css and the same components come out as a different store. No hex values live in components. Eight reference brands prove the range.

        
    
          
  • 8 storefronts live on Vercel
    • 
          
  • 114 brand-consistent product images
    • 
          
  • Next.js 15, Tailwind preset, Turborepo
    • 
          
  • Catalog snapshots, zero cross-brand leaks

How StoreKit is built

        A design contract, then faithful brand apps.
        
The shared package is a Tailwind preset plus a token file. Colours are stored as RGB triplets so opacity modifiers still work, a 50 to 900 brand ramp and a neutral ramp cover every surface, and light or dark is a single attribute flip on the same variables.

        
Each brand app is a complete storefront wired to that contract: catalog, product pages, cart, checkout, account, certificates. Product photography is generated from a logo-locked base so the brand mark renders on the glass instead of being pasted on top. Demos serve a local catalog snapshot, so each store stands alone.

        
Status, honestly: eight reference stores are live, the shared package carries the token contract, and extracting the component variants into the package is the next step.

      

      

        
      

    


    

      

        
Peptiful

        One warehouse, many storefronts, one stock.
        
The multi-tenant commerce and telehealth engine I helped found at Built By Design. A shared product list and a single stock count sit under several white-label health brands, each with its own name, storefront and customers. When one shop sells, stock drops for all of them at once.

        
Underneath: patient intake, physician matching, fulfilment and pharmacy backends, SMS automation, and per-brand subaccounts. A new brand goes live in days because the engine already exists. I own the UI and the integration layer.

Operations that run themselves

        EggFlow and Agriful: the farm and the feed mill.
        
My family runs three layer farms, about a hundred thousand birds each. Production arrived as WhatsApp screenshots of Excel, sales lived in a handwritten Urdu register, and knowing who owed what meant calling the sales guy.

        
EggFlow replaced that. The doctor enters production once on a phone. A photo of a handwritten bill fills the sales form. Cash, online and credit split themselves, every rupee owed is tracked by age, and the day only closes when the numbers reconcile. Eighty-seven tests pin the paper numbers.

        
Agriful is the same model drawn for a feed mill: dealer orders over WhatsApp, a credit check before the truck moves, collections that chase themselves. It is a proposal with a full service blueprint, not a product yet, and I say so on the page.

Healthpedia

        A family health record humans can read and agents can safely write to.
        
It started as a hand-maintained PDF for my grandmother in Lahore: three doctors, handwritten prescriptions, conflicting plans, one page of truth that someone had to keep current by hand.

        
Now an agent inside Claude or ChatGPT reads the record and files proposed changes from voice notes and prescription photos. Every proposed field carries a confidence score and its evidence, a cropped region of the photo or the exact sentence from the note. Nothing touches the record until a family member approves it in the queue. Agents propose, humans publish.

        
  • Next.js, Postgres, Vercel Blob
  • MCP server with bearer auth
  • Confidence and evidence on every field
